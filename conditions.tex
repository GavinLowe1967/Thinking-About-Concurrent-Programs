\section{Summary}

In this chapter, we have studied how to use conditions to allow one thread to
wait until it receives a signal from another thread.  Each |Condition| object
is associated with a |Lock|.  A thread that performs |await| must hold the
lock, but it releases the lock as part of the |await|.  A thread that
performs a |signal| must also hold the lock; it retains the lock after
performing the |signal|.  The thread that receives the signal must wait to
reobtain the lock, possibly competing with other threads.

Conditions are useful for implementing a partial operation on a datatype: a
thread can wait on the condition until the precondition holds; and a thread
that makes the precondition true can signal on the condition.  They are also
useful for implementing synchronisation objects: a thread can wait for another
thread with which to synchronise; and then the latter thread can signal.  We
have seen examples of both; the exercises include more examples.

The normal reason for a thread to have to wait is that a relevant property
does not hold: either a precondition of a partial operation does not hold, or
the thread has to wait for another thread with which to synchronise. 
Normally, the corresponding signal indicates that the property does hold
at the point of the signal.  However, it is often the case that some third
thread might run---before the first thread reobtains the lock---and falsify the
property again: in such cases, the thread that receives the signal needs
to recheck the property.  Even when this is not the case, it is normally a
good idea to assert the property, as a form of documentation, but also to
catch any behaviours you hadn't thought about.

Designing synchronisation objects, in particular, can be tricky.  Be clear
about the order in which steps should be taken, e.g.~the order of reads and
writes of shared variables, and signals between threads.  It is often best to
enforce a fixed order, rather than allowing multiple different orders: it
means less to think about.  It is normally necessary to use shared variables
to record whether a thread of a particular role is waiting, or how many such
threads are waiting.
